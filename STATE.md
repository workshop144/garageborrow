Open Lows, each with its reason:

- GB-R1-3, accepted risk: anyone who knows a member's number can call Cognito InitiateAuth 10 times and stop that member's sign-in codes until the next UTC day. Reason: it is the price of a hard per-phone SMS spend cap. The trigger sees no caller IP or device, so it cannot tell the member's attempt from an attacker's, and any per-phone budget an outsider can spend they can also exhaust; removing the cap reopens SMS toll fraud on the operator's bill. Cognito threat protection (advanced security) would see the caller but is a paid tier, so that is an operator decision. Limited by the 30-day refresh token: since GB-F5-1 the PWA keeps it until sign-out and restores the session on launch, so a signed-in member needs no code and the lockout only reaches a member signing in fresh (new device, after sign-out, or after 30 days). Visible through the `sms-capped` saved query. Revisit if members report missing codes, or add a non-SMS factor (passkeys) later.
- Borrow-request races (PR #42 review): concurrent requests with different Idempotency-Keys can pass the one-pending and two-open checks. Reason: bounded by the 20/day request counter and the 10/day owner ping bucket; the fix (a `PENDING#` marker in the same TransactWrite) is planned with the owner request UI.
